Example 3 — Lux fulgebit

The entrance chant of the dawn Mass at Christmas. A chant of half-light, all delicacy : the perfect occasion to hear the nuances of duration.

Genre
Introit (entrance chant)
Occasion
Mass at dawn on Christmas Day (in aurora), in the early morning of 25 December
Text
After Isaiah (Is 9, 2 and 9, 6) and psalm 92
Mode
Mode VIII (sol mode, peaceful and settled)
Style
Neumatic and measured : ideal for observing durations
In the Triplex
p. 44
Listen to the chant

As you listen, spot the settled notes and the flowing ones.

The text and its translation

Latin
English
Lux fulgebit hodie super nos :
A light shall shine upon us today ;
quia natus est nobis Dominus :
for a Lord is born to us ;
et vocabitur Admirabilis, Deus,
Princeps pacis, Pater futuri sæculi :
and he shall be called Wonderful, God,
Prince of peace, Father of the world to come ;
cujus regni non erit finis.
and his reign shall have no end.

The real subject here : the duration of the notes

So far we have asked « how many notes ? » and « do they go up or down ? ». Lux fulgebit invites us to a subtler question : « how long does each note last ? ». For in chant, not all notes have the same duration : some are light and fleeting, others settle and broaden.

Definition — short note, lengthened note

Chant alternates short notes (the ordinary, light flow) and lengthened notes, on which the voice lingers for a moment. This is not a « square » rhythm like a military march : it is a supple breathing, guided by the meaning of the text.

Where do you read these durations ?

This is precisely the area where the old notations of the Triplex shine :

  • The episema (small stroke) above a St. Gall neume : « settle, broaden ».
  • The letter t (tenete) : « hold the note ».
  • The letter c (celeriter) : on the contrary, « pass quickly, do not lean ».
  • In the notation of Laon, the very shape of the stroke (rounded, « hooked »…) already distinguishes short from long with remarkable precision.
A very simple exercise

On the word Lux (the « light ») and then on fulgebit (« will shine »), ask yourself : does the upper layer (Laon) or the lower one (St. Gall) tell me to settle the voice, or to flow on ? You will find that the moments of leaning often fall on the words the text wants to highlight : the light, the birth, the peace.

Key point for this chant

With Lux fulgebit we move from the question « which notes ? » to the question « what duration, what leaning ? ». This is the heart of what makes the Graduale Triplex so precious : the two old layers make audible what square notation alone leaves mute.
